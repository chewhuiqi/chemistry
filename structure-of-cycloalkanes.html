Cycloalkanes: 3D shape and ring strain

Drag each model to rotate it. Compare the C–C–C bond angles with the preferred tetrahedral angle of 109.5°. The models use source-guided conformations rather than the flat polygon drawings normally used in skeletal formulae.

Reference guide used for the teaching values: Chemistry LibreTexts, Wade, “Cycloalkanes and Ring Strain”. Bond-angle labels are approximate because real rings vibrate and, except for cyclopropane, are not flat regular polygons.
CyclopropaneC3H6
Drag to rotate
Ring: 3-membered
C–C–C: 60°
Shape: planar equilateral triangle
Rotation: essentially none
High ring strain. The 60° C–C–C angle is far from 109.5°. The ring also has substantial eclipsing / torsional strain.
Source total strain27.7 kcal mol⁻¹
CyclobutaneC4H8
Drag to rotate
Ring: 4-membered
C–C–C: ≈88° in puckered form
Shape: folded “butterfly”
Why pucker? reduces eclipsing
High ring strain, but slightly less than cyclopropane. Puckering reduces torsional strain, but the C–C–C angles remain far from 109.5°.
Source total strain26.3 kcal mol⁻¹
CyclopentaneC5H10
Drag to rotate
Ring: 5-membered
C–C–C: ≈107–108°
Shape: envelope-like, non-planar
Effect: little angle strain
Much less ring strain. Its C–C–C angles are close to 109.5°. Puckering removes much of the eclipsing strain, although some torsional strain remains.
Source total strain6.5 kcal mol⁻¹
CyclohexaneC6H12
Drag to rotate
Ring: 6-membered
C–C–C: ≈109.5° in idealised chair
Shape: chair conformation
Effect: nearly staggered bonds
Minimal ring strain. The chair conformation keeps bond angles close to the tetrahedral value and virtually eliminates angle and eclipsing strain.
Source total strain0.4 kcal mol⁻¹
